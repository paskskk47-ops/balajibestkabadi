01 You Earn Real Cash — Every Month

Recycling scrap isn't charity — it's a side income. An average Jaipur household generates 15–25 kg of mixed scrap monthly: newspapers (₹17/kg), plastic (₹12/kg), iron (₹32/kg), and more. That adds up to ₹200–₹500 per month — or ₹2,400 to ₹6,000 annually — just from waste you'd otherwise throw away. Regular sellers with bulk renovation scrap or e-waste can earn significantly more.

02 Reduce Jaipur's Landfill Crisis

Jaipur's landfills are critically overloaded. The city generates over 1,200 tonnes of solid waste daily, and a significant portion is recyclable material that could be recovered. When you sell your scrap to a certified kabadi wala, that material is processed by recycling plants instead of rotting in landfills. Every kilogram you recycle is one less kilogram contaminating Rajasthan's groundwater.

03 Conserve Natural Resources & Energy

Producing new aluminium from raw bauxite ore consumes 95% more energy than recycling used aluminium. Recycling one tonne of paper saves 17 trees and 26,000 litres of water. When you hand your scrap to a kabadi wala in Jaipur, you're directly reducing mining activity, forest destruction, and industrial water consumption across India — a real, measurable environmental contribution.

04 Declutter Your Home — Completely Free

Scrap piles up silently. Old newspapers in the storeroom, broken appliances in the garage, worn-out iron pipes from the renovation — these take up valuable space and attract pests. A professional kabadi pickup service clears all of it in one visit, at no cost to you. Balaji Kabadi Wala provides free doorstep collection with zero logistics fees. Your home, decluttered — and you get paid for it.

05 Support Jaipur's Local Circular Economy

Every rupee from your scrap sale circulates within Jaipur's local economy. The materials we collect are processed in Sitapura's industrial zone, supporting local recycling factories, transport workers, and sorters. Selling to a local kabadi wala means supporting hundreds of Jaipur families — not exporting waste to distant corporate facilities. It's a community-level circular economy working at its best.
